- **(a) Doctrine touched:** the Gate B H3 hazard entry (resolved-by-removal rather than fix); Q5's H3 non-inheritance clause (becomes vacuously satisfied — a clause about a path that no longer exists); the H3 boundary study's invariants (remain standing as the explanation of why nothing may informally replace the retired path); the `/promote` surface's documented semantics (parameter retirement is a future implementation question, not performed here). The operator-only admission floor is **not** touched: the floor governs authorization, not override, and survives unchanged.
- **(b) Tests/invariants needing future re-locking:** the `6a4da8f` bypass inventory (snapshot pins current surfaces; removal would change the terrain it locks); any Gate B characterization that references the force path's existence; authority-lane assertions that enumerate promotion paths.
- **(c) Surfaces remaining closed:** all of them — retirement opens nothing; Gate B reopening would still be required to *perform* the removal, as its own gated slice.
- **(d) Risks reduced:** the self-laundering hazard is eliminated at the root; transmissible-authority risk from force goes to zero; the mark-becomes-rank failure mode loses its subject.
- **(e) Risks newly exposed:** exceptional corrections would have to occur through ordinary governed channels or not at all; the record would need to guard against **informal, unwitnessed out-of-band intervention** (direct file/store surgery) becoming a de-facto override without Q2/Q3 evidence — the pressure the path currently absorbs does not vanish with the path.
- **(f) Unanswered:** whether removal is cleanly implementable (deferred to a future gated slice); the disposition of historically forced rows, if any exist (provenance archaeology, deferred); whether any doctrine should name the informal-intervention guard explicitly (a future frame's question).
