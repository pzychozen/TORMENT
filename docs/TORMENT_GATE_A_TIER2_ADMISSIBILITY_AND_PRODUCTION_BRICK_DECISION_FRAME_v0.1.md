## 6. Admissibility analysis — does this frame conclude a tiny production brick?

**No.** Source-grounded reasons, not preference:

- The three postponed surfaces were postponed for stated reasons that still hold
  (Layer 4 closure §5): **ArchiveStore** — HTTP cannot carry a `CandidateShapedValue`
  and archive text self-defends; **`links`** — structurally open but
  production-unreachable; **`update_payload`** — callers internally constructed and
  the all-values guard had the wrong scanning shape. None of these is reached by a
  candidate today.
- A negative type-only brick only matters once *something can emit a candidate into
  that surface*. **No candidate producer exists or is authorized.** Adding a sixth
  refusal now hardens a path nothing can travel — marginal value, and it risks
  reading as a second brick series the closure and the Seam B characterization
  explicitly refused.
- The real remaining risk is not an unguarded ordinary ingress; it is that the
  wall has **no positive admission mechanism** (§4). A tiny brick does not move that
  bar; only Fork 3's design path does.

`[CONCLUSION]` This frame concludes **no specific tiny production brick is
independently admissible now.** Fork 4 remains unselected; the residual brick
*question* (Fork 2) stays an operator-gated triage item, deliberately not a series.
